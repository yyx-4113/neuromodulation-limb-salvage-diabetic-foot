**Background**: Guidelines for spinal cord stimulation (SCS) in diabetic foot (DF) increasingly carry explicit evidence grades, yet development quality, and whether the recommendations match the evidence they cite, are seldom examined together. We appraised a 2026 Chinese expert consensus on SCS for DF with three complementary instruments and asked whether its self-assigned evidence levels were supported by the sources it cites.

**Methods**: In this cross-sectional evaluation of one consensus, AGREE II was applied by two independent appraisers, RIGHT (22 topics/35 subitems) by one reviewer, and GRADE certainty for nine outcomes across six recommendations by two appraisers using masked templates and the consensus' own cited literature; the global quality rating was one appraiser's judgement. Agreement used exact agreement, κ and ICC(2,1) (AGREE II) and exact final-certainty and domain agreement (GRADE). Cited-source claims were checked against full texts where obtainable.

**Results**: AGREE II domain scores ranged from 41.7% to 83.3% for appraiser 1 and 33.3% to 72.2% for appraiser 2; the three highest-scoring domains matched, but editorial independence ranked fourth and last respectively. Exact item agreement was 39.1% (9/23), with weighted κ 0.651 and ICC(2,1) 0.853 (95%CI 0.663–0.941); the lower unweighted κ (0.258) reflects equal weighting of one-point and five-point disagreements on this seven-point scale. RIGHT classified 15 subitems Yes, 16 Unclear, 4 No. In the GRADE reconstruction, eight of nine outcomes were very low certainty and one low; indirectness (DF versus painful diabetic neuropathy; limb salvage versus pain endpoints) was cross-cutting, and four of six recommendations had no usable evidence body. Two appraisers agreed on final certainty for all nine outcomes; per-domain agreement ranged from 44.4% (indirectness) to 100% (inconsistency), the latter reflecting uniformly 'not assessable' judgements. Four quantitative and one qualitative claim cited one reference containing neither SCS nor the claimed numbers.

**Conclusions**: The consensus reports scope and recommendations clearly but has substantial limitations in rigour, applicability and evidence linkage. Independent GRADE appraisers reached the same certainty conclusions through partly different downgrading paths. The findings support methodological revision rather than a judgement on SCS efficacy.
